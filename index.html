Voter Turnout Demographic Visualizations

U.S. Census and election data, 1978–2024 · Built with Python, pandas and Plotly

Abstract

This website holds visualizations comparing trends related to the voting demographic in the United States between different races-- White, Black, Asian, and Hispanic-- income ranges, and how these two qualities affect the voting demographic for midterm and primary elections. While there are multiple visuals that show trends over time, there is an emphasis on the 2024 primary elections.

To summarize the findings, there seems to be no strong correlation between a race's annual income and the percentage citizen population of that race who voted. Consistently, Asians have the highest median income, followed by White, Hispanic, and Black. However, the highest racial voting percentage demographic throughout 1978-2024 is consistently White, followed by Black, with Asian and Hispanic consistently competing for last. Interestingly, when race and income are taken into consideration separately, voting percentage does go up given increased annual income, and there are clear distinctions in voting rates between races.

NOTE: throughout the datasets used, distinctions are made between people who are recorded as multiple races. For example, a person recorded as white can have only recorded white as their race (single race) or white along with some other race. However, one of the datasets only uses single race, so for consistency, all information regarding race is gathered from "single" race data samples.

Conclusion

Regarding percentage of voting population, the interaction between race and income does not seem significant. However, taken into account individually, both race and income do seem significant: Whites have the highest voter percentage, followed by Blacks, and Asians having an average voting percentage only 1.94% higher than Hispanics (from 1990-2024). People who reported having an annual family income of $150,000 or over have a 35.9% higher voter percentage than those with a family income under $10,000.
